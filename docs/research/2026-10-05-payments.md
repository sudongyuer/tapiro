## 1. Malaysia regulation

### 1.1 What counts as e-money

- **Definition (primary).** Under the Financial Services (Designated Payment Instruments) Order 2013, P.U.(A) 202, para 2(d), e-money is "any payment instrument, whether tangible or intangible, that (i) stores funds electronically in exchange of funds paid to the issuer; and (ii) is able to be used as a means of making payment to any person other than the issuer". Source: https://lom.agc.gov.my/ilims/upload/portal/akta/outputp/pua_20130629_P.U.%20(A)%20202.pdf
- The definition has two limbs, and an instrument must meet both:
  1. **Funded with money paid to the issuer.** Points that can only be earned and are never bought fail this limb. [I]
  2. **Usable to pay someone other than the issuer.** Points that pay other users for tasks meet this limb. Points spent only on Tapiro's own perks do not. [I]
- **Licensing.** Issuing a designated payment instrument needs BNM approval under FSA s.11. The LPEM Order below exempts issuers "from the application of paragraph 8(1)(b) and section 11 of the Act". Source: P.U.(A) 463/2024, para 3, https://lom.agc.gov.my/ilims/upload/portal/akta/outputp/2596772/PUA%20463.pdf
- **Limited Purpose E-Money (Exemption) Order 2024, P.U.(A) 463/2024.** In force since 2 Jan 2025. It replaced the "limited purpose EMI" provisions that BNM removed from the revised e-money policy document of 31 Jan 2025. Sources: same PDF; https://www.bnm.gov.my/gazette-order; Skrine alert (secondary) http://www.skrine.com/insights/alerts/february-2025/bank-negara-malaysia-issues-revised-policy-documen
  - **Category 1 (purchasing goods or services).** All three criteria apply:
    - (a) usable "solely … for the purchase of goods or services within a single premises or a single business network under a single brand operating in Malaysia";
    - (b) average daily outstanding liabilities and average monthly transaction value each no more than **RM1,000,000** per issuer;
    - (c) wallet limit no more than **RM500 per user**.
  - **Conditions for Category 1** (para 4(1)(a)):
    - comply with PDPA;
    - clearly disclose that the instrument is not BNM-approved e-money;
    - provide complaints and dispute resolution;
    - file an annual notification and undertaking with BNM;
    - file annual statistics attested by an **external auditor**;
    - "facilitate the transfer of any amount of stored funds … into any bank account of the user". This means a Category 1 balance must be redeemable to the user's bank account.
  - **Category 2 (rewards).** Funded by a person under an arrangement with the issuer, usable only with persons other than that funder, and held in a separate account. Categories 3 and 4 cover refunds and telcos.
  - **Fit for Tapiro (A):** whether a P2P marketplace where users pay *other users* counts as "a single business network under a single brand" is **[U]**. I read it as aimed at a merchant's own outlets, so P2P payments probably fall outside it [I]. Even if it fits, the RM500 cap and the audit and reporting duties apply.
- **BNM's e-money policy document.** The PD and FAQ PDFs linked from https://www.bnm.gov.my/-/pd-emoney (PD-eMoney-202302.pdf, FAQ-eMoney-202212.pdf) returned **HTTP 404** today. I could not read the current PD's safeguarding and wallet-tier rules. **[U]**
- **Licensed non-bank e-money issuers.** BNM's directory lists 47 entities, including TNG Digital, ShopeePay, BigPay, PayPal Pte Ltd, Wise Payments Malaysia, LazadaPay and Airwallex (Malaysia). Source: https://www.bnm.gov.my/non-bank-e-money-issuers

### 1.2 Holding customer funds, escrow, merchant acquiring, payment facilitators

- **Policy Document on Merchant Acquiring Services**, BNM/RH/PD 028-119, 15 Sep 2021. https://www.bnm.gov.my/documents/20124/943361/PD_Merchant_Acquiring_Services.pdf
  - **para 2.1:** the PD applies to acquirers registered under FSA ss.17(1) and 18 that contract with merchants, settle (or instruct settlement of) funds to them, and are direct participants of payment networks.
  - **Definitions:**
    - "payment facilitator": "an entity that is appointed by an acquirer to perform merchant acquiring services on behalf of the acquirer";
    - "merchant": includes "merchants acquired by a payment facilitator on behalf of an acquirer".
  - **para 10.4:** settlement to merchants should take no more than **2 working days (physical) / 5 working days (e-commerce)** from receipt.
  - **para 10.5:** settlement funds must sit in a dedicated deposit account.
  - **para 15.2:** a payment facilitator must:
    - do due diligence on its merchants;
    - keep settlement funds in a designated bank account;
    - have dispute resolution in place.
  - **para 15.3:** the acquirer is liable if the facilitator fails to settle.
  - **Implication [I]:** if Tapiro collected payers' money into its own account and paid helpers later, it would be acting like a payment facilitator or remitter, and possibly an e-money issuer, without registration. The licensed provider has to be the party that holds and moves the funds.
- **Stripe and escrow.** Stripe "doesn't provide escrow services". Platforms can delay payouts with manual payouts for up to **90 days** in "all other countries", which includes Malaysia. https://docs.stripe.com/connect/manual-payouts

### 1.3 AML/CFT (AMLA) KYC

- **BNM PD "AML/CFT and TFS for Financial Institutions"**, BNM/RH/PD 030-3. The copy fetched is stamped "Issued on 31 December 2019". **[U]** It may have been revised since. https://amlcft.bnm.gov.my/documents/6312201/6321216/AML_CFT_TFS_PD.pdf
- **para 14A.11.5:** standard CDD is required for e-money accounts if any of these apply:
  - account limit of RM5,000 or more;
  - monthly transactions of RM5,000 or more;
  - annual transactions of RM60,000 or more;
  - payments outside Malaysia;
  - cross-border wire transfers;
  - cash withdrawal.
- **para 14A.11.6:** simplified CDD is allowed for account limits of RM3,000 to RM4,999 if all conditions are met (domestic only, no cash-out, and so on).
- **para 14A.11.7:** simplified CDD is the minimum below RM3,000 when the account can make domestic transfers.
- **para 14A.10.3:** even simplified CDD needs full name, **NRIC or passport number**, address, date of birth and nationality.
- **para 14A.9.1:** standard CDD also needs occupation and employer.
- **Conflict:** this collides directly with the rule "collect no IC/passport/student ID numbers" whenever Tapiro itself is the regulated entity.

## 2. Apple App Review Guidelines (https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/, fetched 2026-10-05; the page shows no last-updated date)

- **3.1.1:** "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions, in-game currencies, … ) you must use in-app purchase." Also: "Any credits or in-game currencies purchased via in-app purchase may not expire". IAP gifts "may only be refunded to the original purchaser and may not be exchanged."
- **3.1.3(d) Person-to-Person Services:** "If your app enables the purchase of real-time person-to-person services between two individuals (for example tutoring students, medical consultations, real estate tours, or fitness training), you may use purchase methods other than in-app purchase … One-to-few and one-to-many real-time services must use in-app purchase."
- **3.1.3(e) Goods and Services Outside of the App:** "If your app enables people to purchase physical goods or services that will be consumed outside of the app, you **must** use purchase methods other than in-app purchase to collect those payments, such as Apple Pay or traditional credit card entry."
- **3.1.3(g):** "Digital purchases for content that is experienced or consumed in an app, including buying advertisements to display in the same app (such as sales of **"boosts" for posts** in a social media app) must use in-app purchase."
- **3.2.1(vii):** person-to-person monetary gifts may skip IAP if optional and 100% goes to the receiver. **3.2.1(viii):** "money management" apps "should be submitted by the financial institution performing such services and must have necessary licensing".
- **3.1.5:** cryptocurrencies. Not relevant.
- There is **no 3.1.3(g) about P2P payments**. The current 3.1.3(g) is "Advertising Management Apps", and it contains the boosts sentence quoted above.
- **Commission:** the Small Business Program rate is 15% for developers with up to USD 1M in proceeds. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/

**Conclusions** (all [I] from the quoted text):
- **Topping up points for real-world errands:** this is payment for services consumed outside the app, so 3.1.3(e) requires non-IAP. IAP must not be used for it.
- **Points that also unlock boosts, pins or cosmetics:** these are digital and fall under 3.1.1 and 3.1.3(g), so they must use IAP. A single currency that both pays for real-world services and unlocks digital perks mixes the two regimes and is a review risk. Keep them as separate ledgers.
- **Cashing out IAP-purchased credits:** the guidelines don't say this in so many words. IAP credits are sold as non-expiring consumables, and IAP gifts "may not be exchanged". Cashing them out would also turn Tapiro into a stored-value issuer. Treat it as not allowed. **[U]**
- **Points usable only to pay for real-world services between users:** the payment rails are fine (non-IAP is required under 3.1.3(e), and permitted for tutoring under 3.1.3(d)). The licensing problem in section 1 remains, and 3.2.1(viii) puts weight on the licensed institution being the party that does it.
- **Earned-only coins that are never sold** (design D): no purchase happens, so 3.1.1 IAP is not triggered by the coins. Paid perks (boost, pin, mascot cosmetics) must be IAP under 3.1.1 and 3.1.3(g).

## 4. Chinese wallets (Alipay+ / WeChat Pay)

- **Alipay+ wallets:** Alipay+ connects wallets including **Alipay (Chinese mainland)**, TNG and others (search snippet of https://www.alipayplus.com/mobile-payment-provider-list/). **[U]** Merchants settle in a single "merchant settlement currency". https://docs.alipayplus.com/alipayplus/alipayplus/glossary/v1
- **Settlement currency:** Stripe Alipay in MY settles in MYR. https://docs.stripe.com/payments/alipay Xendit WeChat Pay MY is in MYR. https://docs.xendit.co/docs/my-wechatpay
- **Alipay restrictions** (via Stripe, https://stripe.com/legal/alipay):
  - prohibited: crowdfunding, financial intermediation, illegal encashment, and "Services to facilitate plagiarism and examination fraud";
  - restricted (needs approval): "Prepaid cards and top-ups (including gift cards and other stored value cards)" and "Payment platform".
- **WeChat Pay Proper Use Rules** (https://pay.weixin.qq.com/index.php/public/wechatpay_en/proper_rule):
  - forbidden: "Open-loop prepaid cards" and "Academic fraud, essay ghostwriting, proxy test taking";
  - restricted (pre-approval needed): "Single-purpose or other closed-loop prepaid cards and top-ups (including gift cards and other stored value cards)" and "Fees payment agency platform".
- **What this means for Tapiro [I]:**
  - Using Alipay or WeChat Pay to *top up Tapiro points* (design A) runs into the prepaid / top-up restrictions.
  - "Study questions" bounties must be framed as tutoring or explanation. Doing someone's homework or exams is a forbidden category on both networks, and probably a university misconduct issue too.

## 5. Tax and student-pass work rules

### Tax (brief)

- From 1 Jul 2025, service tax scope expanded to "leasing or rental, construction, financial services, private healthcare, education, and beauty services". https://www.mof.gov.my/portal/en/news/press-release/targeted-revision-of-sales-tax-rate-and-expansion-of-service-tax-scope-effective-1-july-2025
- Foreign service providers of digital services, which includes operators of online platforms for services, must register for service tax when sales to Malaysian consumers exceed **RM500,000 a year** (search snippet of the RMCD FSP guide). **[U]** https://mysst.customs.gov.my/assets/document/Industry%20Guides/GI/Guide%20on%20Digital%20Service%20by%20FSP_V2_01022021.pdf
- Whether a Malaysian-incorporated Tapiro's platform fee is a taxable service, and how helpers' income is taxed, is **[U]**. Both depend on where the entity is incorporated. Ask the lawyer or tax agent.

### Student pass and paid work (the critical constraint)

- **Immigration Department (IMI):**
  - part-time work is only for UA/IPTS students ("Language Centres, Training/Skills Centre, and Accredited Centres ARE NOT PERMITTED");
  - "limited to 20 hours per week, only at approved locations": restaurants, petrol stations, mini markets, hotels and university/college areas;
  - the application needs a **job offer letter**, a support letter from the institution and a passport copy;
  - "Any student found violating regulations will have their Student Pass revoked."
  - Source: https://www.imi.gov.my/index.php/en/main-services/pass/student-pass/
- **EMGS FAQ:** "maximum of 20 hours per week **ONLY during semester breaks or holidays of more than 7 days** at restaurants, petrol kiosks, mini markets and hotels". No cashier roles. Prior approval from Immigration is needed, applied for through the institution. https://educationmalaysia.gov.my/get-in-touch/faq
- **The two sources differ.** IMI says part-time work is allowed "during their study period"; EMGS says "ONLY during semester breaks". **[U]** Which one is current needs confirming.
- **Neither source mentions freelance, gig or P2P work, or self-employment** (EMGS FAQ fetched; no entry). Errands, moving help and paid companionship for other individuals are not an approved sector, and there is no employer to issue a job offer letter.
- **Conclusion [I]:** a student-pass holder who earns cash bounties is very likely doing unauthorised work, and the stated penalty is pass revocation. A paid marketplace where most helpers are Chinese students on student passes exposes those users.
- No primary source addresses **non-monetary** reciprocity (time-banking). **[U]**, and the top lawyer question.

### Recommendation: D, launched as "coins + IAP perks"; the cash track stays switched off until a lawyer clears it

1. **The coins stay out of e-money by definition.** They are never bought and never cashed out, so they fail limb (i) of P.U.(A) 202. That needs no exemption filing, no audit and no KYC, and it keeps the no-ID rule intact.
2. **Commitment without money.** Locking coins when a bounty is posted and releasing them on completion gives the "prepay" commitment the founder wants.
3. **Revenue that Apple already allows.** Boost, pin and cosmetics go through IAP, which 3.1.3(g) requires anyway.
4. **The biggest legal risk is not a payments problem.** For student-pass helpers it is immigration. A, B and the cash half of D all pay cash to student-pass holders for unapproved work. Shipping in-app cash before that is cleared would put users' passes at risk.
5. **Escrow needs a licensed provider.** Neither Stripe nor Tapiro can lawfully "escrow". When the cash track is built, it must be the provider's hold mechanism (Curlec "on hold", or Stripe manual payouts of up to 90 days). It should be limited to helpers who can legally earn (citizens, PRs, pass types with work rights), confirmed by the provider's KYC and not by Tapiro storing IDs.

**Guardrails for D** (each one keeps a coin out of the e-money definition or off the payment networks' prohibited lists):
- No path from money to coins: no purchase, no "cash bounty paid in coins", no coin bundles with IAP.
- No coins to money, and no user-to-user coin transfer outside task completion.
- Prohibit coin resale in the Terms.
- Keep IAP perks on a separate SKU. Coins must not be sold through it.
- Keep study bounties to tutoring and explanation (Alipay and WeChat Pay prohibited lists; the 3.1.3(d) tutoring example).

## 7. Open questions for a Malaysian lawyer

1. **Time-bank coins and student passes.** Does helping in exchange for non-cash, non-transferable coins count as "work" or "employment" for a Student Pass holder under the Immigration Act and Regulations? Does the starter grant change that?
2. **IMI vs EMGS.** Which statement on student part-time work is current ("during their study period" or "ONLY during semester breaks")? Can student pass holders ever earn from P2P gig tasks?
3. **E-money definition.** Confirm that earned-only, non-purchasable, non-cashable coins fall outside P.U.(A) 202 para 2(d). Does anything change if coins can be redeemed for Tapiro's own digital perks?
4. **LPEM Category 1.** Does "single business network under a single brand" cover a P2P marketplace? This only matters if A is ever reconsidered.
5. **Platform fees on a provider's marketplace.** Is Tapiro a "payment facilitator" or otherwise regulated if it never touches funds but sets splits and holds via the provider's API?
